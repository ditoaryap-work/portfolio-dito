## Context and goal

Buying a home involves several decisions: estimating affordability, checking a property, and planning work after the purchase. salesone.id was built as one place to prepare for several of those steps.

The available notes do not include early user research or the reason behind each feature. This story therefore stays with the documented product scope.

## What we built

The product brings together mortgage simulations, a purchase checklist, a home inspection tool, and renovation planning. These tools help users move from an initial cost estimate toward inspection and follow-up plans.

## My role and decisions

I developed the frontend and backend with one project partner. The product brought several preparation tools into one place so users could follow a connected set of steps.

The illustration above shows the intended feature sequence. It is a map of product scope, not a record of user behavior inside the application.

## Results and impact

The product reached about 130 users in under two weeks. The project notes do not say whether this means registered users, active users, or people who completed a particular flow, so I present it as the reported user count.

## Trade-offs and questions to measure

A product with several tools offers broad coverage, but the user count alone does not show which tool helped most. A next iteration could track repeat use, where people leave, and whether a plan leads to an inspection or renovation.

## What I learned

When a product includes several steps, the path between features matters as much as each feature. Early usage shows reach; behavioral data can help explain what to prioritize next.
